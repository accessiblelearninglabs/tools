Annotated reading list

The 55 works the accessibility and Universal Design for Learning resources rest on, each with what it is used for and where it is contested.

How each entry is set out. Used for states the work the source does in these resources. Where it is contested records a documented limitation or critique, attributed to the person who made it. Position records the reading taken here where it departs from the source.

Selection. Every work below is cited somewhere in the resources. Nothing appears here that is not used, and the list is not a survey of the field.

Availability. 16 of the 55 are open access. The remainder are held in most academic libraries.

9 works

Disability studies and justice

The scholarship that supplies the models, and the accounts of ableism and of participation that the design work rests on.

  • Book

    Oliver, M. (1990). The Politics of Disablement. Macmillan.

    Used for

    The foundational statement of the social model, and the premise on which a barrier is treated as a property of the environment rather than of the person.

    Where it is contested

    Shakespeare (2013) argues that strong formulations sever disability from the body entirely, and so cannot account for impairment effects that persist once barriers are removed.

  • Book

    Shakespeare, T. (2013). Disability Rights and Wrongs Revisited (2nd ed.). Routledge.

    Used for

    The standard critical reassessment of the strong social model, and the source of the interactional position the resources take.

    Where it is contested

    Critics within disability studies argue that the reassessment concedes ground to individualised accounts of disability while the political work of the social model is unfinished.

  • Article

    Retief, M., & Letšosa, R. (2018). “Models of Disability: A Brief Overview.” HTS Teologiese Studies / Theological Studies, 74(1).

    Used for

    A short comparative overview of the models, used as the reference for the models group in the glossary.

    Where it is contested

    A summary rather than an original analysis; each model receives a brief treatment and the comparison is not systematic.

  • Book

    Dolmage, J. T. (2017). Academic Ableism: Disability and Higher Education. University of Michigan Press.

    Used for

    Locates ableism in the architecture, admissions practices and routine habits of higher education rather than in individual attitude.

    Where it is contested

    The claims about institutional structure are argued from example and from rhetorical analysis, and the evidence base is North American.

  • Book

    Titchkosky, T. (2011). The Question of Access: Disability, Space, Meaning. University of Toronto Press.

    Used for

    Examines how institutional language positions access as an exceptional request rather than an ordinary condition of participation.

    Where it is contested

    The method is interpretive analysis of institutional discourse, which yields readings of how access is spoken about rather than findings about what institutions do.

  • Book

    Kafer, A. (2013). Feminist, Queer, Crip. Indiana University Press.

    Used for

    A political and relational account of disability that treats imagined futures as contested ground, used where cure narratives shape institutional expectations.

    Where it is contested

    The argument is developed through feminist and queer theory and offers little that translates directly into institutional procedure.

  • Book

    Charlton, J. I. (1998). Nothing About Us Without Us: Disability Oppression and Empowerment. University of California Press.

    Used for

    The source of the participation principle that sets the threshold for co-design and consultation.

    Where it is contested

    The cross-national evidence rests on interviews conducted in the early 1990s, and the movement it documents has since been criticised from within for centring white activists.

  • Book

    Goffman, E. (1963). Stigma: Notes on the Management of Spoiled Identity. Prentice-Hall.

    Used for

    Explains why people who are entitled to an accommodation decline to request one, which is the basis for designing out the disclosure requirement.

    Where it is contested

    The 1963 framing treats stigma as something the individual manages, which places the labour of concealment with the person rather than with the institution producing the stigma.

  • Guidance

    United Spinal Association. Disability Etiquette: Tips on Interacting with People with Disabilities.

    Used for

    Practical guidance on interaction, used in the etiquette and communication resource.

    Where it is contested

    Practitioner guidance from a single United States organisation, not peer-reviewed, and a list of tips can substitute a script for the judgement it is meant to support.

11 works

Law, rights instruments and governance

Canadian statute and case law, the international and regional instruments, and the information-governance and research-ethics rules that bound practice.

  • Case lawOpen access

    British Columbia (Public Service Employee Relations Commission) v. BCGSEU, [1999] 3 S.C.R. 3, 1999 CanLII 652 (SCC).

    Used for

    The unified bona fide requirement test, and the authority for accommodation to the point of undue hardship.

    Where it is contested

    Commentators have observed that the first two steps are treated as readily satisfied in practice, so the analysis rests almost entirely on the third.

  • Legislation

    Human Rights Code, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 210. Province of British Columbia.

    Used for

    The operative statute in this jurisdiction. Section 8 covers services customarily available to the public, which is the provision education falls under; section 13 covers employment.

    Where it is contested

    Enforcement is complaint-driven, which places the work of initiating on the person already facing the barrier.

  • LegislationOpen access

    Accessible Canada Act, S.C. 2019, c. 10.

    Used for

    The federal barrier-removal framework, and the source of the broad statutory definition of a barrier used throughout the resources.

    Where it is contested

    It reaches federally regulated entities only, so most post-secondary institutions in Canada fall outside its scope.

  • Legislation

    Accessible British Columbia Act, S.B.C. 2021, c. 19. Province of British Columbia.

    Used for

    The provincial framework requiring prescribed organisations to maintain an accessibility committee, an accessibility plan and a public feedback mechanism.

    Where it is contested

    It requires planning and consultation rather than outcomes, and prescribes no technical standard against which a plan can be assessed.

  • LegislationOpen access

    Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, as amended, 42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq.; Rehabilitation Act of 1973, Sections 504 and 508, as amended.

    Used for

    The comparison jurisdiction, and the origin of the procurement expectations that reach Canadian institutions through United States vendors.

    Where it is contested

    United States law with a different remedial structure. It does not apply in Canada, and reasoning transplanted from it can misstate the Canadian duty.

  • InstrumentOpen access

    United Nations (2006). Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD).

    Used for

    The rights framework above the domestic statutes, and the source of the obligation to consult disabled people through their representative organisations.

    Where it is contested

    Implementation depends on domestic law, and the Committee's concluding observations are not enforceable against a state party.

  • InstrumentOpen access

    United Nations (1948). Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

    Used for

    The instrument the later disability-specific instruments build on, used for historical framing.

    Where it is contested

    A declaration rather than a treaty, and disability is not among the grounds it names.

  • Instrument

    World Intellectual Property Organization (2013). Marrakesh Treaty to Facilitate Access to Published Works for Persons Who Are Blind, Visually Impaired or Otherwise Print Disabled.

    Used for

    The instrument that makes accessible-format copies lawful to produce and to move across borders, which is what governs alternative formats of published course materials.

    Where it is contested

    Effect depends on ratification and on the domestic copyright exception enacted, so coverage differs between countries.

  • Instrument

    European Union (2012). Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, 2012/C 326/02.

    Used for

    The regional instrument behind European accessibility obligations, cited where EU standards are compared with Canadian ones.

    Where it is contested

    It binds EU institutions and member states implementing EU law, so its relevance in this work is comparative only.

  • Legislation

    Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 165. Province of British Columbia.

    Used for

    Governs what a British Columbia public body may collect, use and disclose, which is what constrains what faculty may be told about a student's accommodation.

    Where it is contested

    It sets limits on disclosure without specifying what an accommodation process should record, so practice varies between institutions under the same statute.

  • PolicyOpen access

    Canadian Institutes of Health Research, Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council, & Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council (2022). Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct for Research Involving Humans — TCPS 2 (2022).

    Used for

    The governing research ethics policy in Canada, and the basis for consented, compensated and scoped participation in user evaluation.

    Where it is contested

    Written for research, so its provisions map imperfectly onto usability evaluation and consultation, which frequently fall outside the definition of research requiring review.

9 works

Standards, assistive technology and design

The technical standards digital work is assessed against, the frameworks for assistive technology decisions, and the design traditions upstream of them.

  • StandardOpen access

    W3C (2023). Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2. W3C Recommendation.

    Used for

    The criterion-level standard digital content is assessed against, and the basis of every conformance position in the resources.

    Where it is contested

    Conformance is assessed criterion by criterion and does not establish that a task can be completed; the W3C states that evaluation tools cannot determine conformance on their own.

  • StandardOpen access

    ETSI/CEN/CENELEC (2021). EN 301 549 V3.2.1: Accessibility Requirements for ICT Products and Services. Harmonised European Standard.

    Used for

    The standard adopted in Canada without modification as CAN/ASC-EN 301 549:2024, extending beyond web content to hardware, software, documents and support services.

    Where it is contested

    V3.2.1 incorporates WCAG 2.1, so the adopted Canadian standard sits a version behind current WCAG.

  • InstrumentOpen access

    Information Technology Industry Council. Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT) and Accessibility Conformance Reports.

    Used for

    The procurement instrument that records conformance criterion by criterion, and the document a vendor accountability process asks for.

    Where it is contested

    Reports are completed by the vendor, and nothing in the format requires that an independent evaluator produced or checked them.

  • Standard

    CSA Group. CSA B651: Accessible Design for the Built Environment. Canadian Standards Association.

    Used for

    The Canadian technical standard for physical space, used where a learning environment is in a room rather than on a screen.

    Where it is contested

    It specifies dimensional and technical requirements and does not reach the social and procedural conditions that determine whether a compliant room is usable.

  • Book

    Cook, A. M., & Polgar, J. M. (2015). Assistive Technologies: Principles and Practice (4th ed.). Elsevier.

    Used for

    The HAAT model, which frames an assistive technology decision as a relationship between person, activity, technology and context.

    Where it is contested

    Written for rehabilitation practice, so its unit of analysis is one person's setup rather than the cohort a course has to serve at once.

  • Framework

    Zabala, J. S. (2005). Ready, SETT, Go! Getting Started with the SETT Framework.

    Used for

    Sequences assistive technology decisions so that the tool is selected last, after student, environments and tasks have been described.

    Where it is contested

    A practitioner framework developed for K–12 special education in the United States, without published empirical validation of the sequence it prescribes.

  • ReportOpen access

    World Health Organization & UNICEF (2022). Global Report on Assistive Technology. World Health Organization.

    Used for

    The global evidence on need and unmet need for assistive products, used where the scale of the gap is at issue.

    Where it is contested

    Estimates are assembled from modelled and self-reported data of uneven quality across countries, so the global figures carry wide uncertainty.

  • GuidanceOpen access

    Inclusive Design Research Centre. The Inclusive Design Guide. OCAD University.

    Used for

    The source of one-size-fits-one and of designing with rather than for, which is the framing the partnership resource uses.

    Where it is contested

    A practitioner guide rather than a research output, and its claims are not accompanied by outcome evidence.

  • Framework

    Connell, B. R., Jones, M., Mace, R., Mueller, J., Mullick, A., Ostroff, E., Sanford, J., Steinfeld, E., Story, M., & Vanderheiden, G. (1997). The Principles of Universal Design, Version 2.0. Center for Universal Design, North Carolina State University.

    Used for

    The seven principles, and the origin of the vocabulary that Universal Design for Learning later borrowed.

    Where it is contested

    Formulated for physical products and environments. The principles were not derived for learning and transfer to it by analogy rather than by evidence.

3 works

Universal Design for Learning

The framework itself, in the versions the resources cite.

  • FrameworkOpen access

    CAST (2024). Universal Design for Learning Guidelines, version 3.0.

    Used for

    The operative version of the framework, and the structure the UDL branch of the resources follows.

    Where it is contested

    Published by the organisation that developed the framework, and version 3.0 postdates the evidence base, which was collected on earlier versions.

  • Book

    Meyer, A., Rose, D. H., & Gordon, D. (2014). Universal Design for Learning: Theory and Practice. CAST Professional Publishing.

    Used for

    The book-length statement of the theory, used where the framework's reasoning rather than its checkpoints is at issue.

    Where it is contested

    King-Sears et al. (2023) report a moderate positive combined effect of g = 0.43 across 20 studies, and reviews consistently note variable study quality and inconsistent operationalisation of what counts as a UDL intervention.

  • Book

    Rose, D. H., & Meyer, A. (2002). Teaching Every Student in the Digital Age: Universal Design for Learning. ASCD.

    Used for

    The original statement of the framework and the source of its three-principle structure.

    Where it is contested

    The mapping of the three principles onto recognition, strategic and affective brain networks is offered as a rationale rather than as a tested mechanism, and later CAST publications rely on it less.

9 works

Learning design, assessment and pedagogy

Backward design, validity theory, the learning sciences, motivation, and the pedagogy the equity-centred resources draw on.

  • Book

    Wiggins, G., & McTighe, J. (2005). Understanding by Design (Expanded 2nd ed.). ASCD.

    Used for

    Backward design, used to hold outcomes, evidence and instruction in alignment so that flexibility can be added without loosening what is assessed.

    Where it is contested

    A planning heuristic rather than a tested intervention; its wide adoption in practice is not matched by direct experimental evidence for the framework itself.

  • Article

    Messick, S. (1995). “Validity of Psychological Assessment: Validation of Inferences from Persons’ Responses and Performances as Scientific Inquiry into Score Meaning.” American Psychologist, 50(9), 741–749.

    Used for

    The unified account of validity, which is what makes an inaccessible assessment a measurement problem rather than a fairness courtesy.

    Where it is contested

    The unified framework is difficult to operationalise, and in practice it is frequently reduced to a checklist of evidence types rather than an argument about an inference.

  • Book

    Mayer, R. E. (2009). Multimedia Learning (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.

    Used for

    The evidence base for presenting multimedia so that it does not impose load unrelated to the material.

    Where it is contested

    Much of the evidence comes from short laboratory studies with undergraduate participants, and generalisation to full courses and to disabled learners is not established.

  • ReportOpen access

    Bransford, J. D., Brown, A. L., & Cocking, R. R. (Eds.). (2000). How People Learn: Brain, Mind, Experience, and School (Expanded ed.). National Academy Press.

    Used for

    The consensus synthesis on learning, transfer and the development of expertise.

    Where it is contested

    Published in 2000, and several of the areas it summarises have moved substantially since; a successor volume was issued in 2018.

  • ArticleOpen access

    Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). “Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being.” American Psychologist, 55(1), 68–78.

    Used for

    Autonomy, competence and relatedness, used where learners attend by choice and engagement cannot be assumed.

    Where it is contested

    The three needs are inferred constructs, and their measurement varies enough across studies that effect sizes are not straightforwardly comparable.

  • Book

    Wenger, E. (1998). Communities of Practice: Learning, Meaning, and Identity. Cambridge University Press.

    Used for

    Explains how practice changes through participation rather than through instruction, which is the basis of the capacity-building work.

    Where it is contested

    The concept was developed descriptively and has been criticised for being applied prescriptively to groups assembled by an institution, which are not the communities the book describes.

  • Book

    Knowles, M. S., Holton, E. F., & Swanson, R. A. (2015). The Adult Learner (8th ed.). Routledge.

    Used for

    Andragogy, used for its assumptions about self-direction, prior experience and readiness in adult cohorts.

    Where it is contested

    Hartree (1984) argued that andragogy lacks an epistemological base and that its assumptions read as prescriptions for what an adult learner should be; the empirical support is thin and the adult–child distinction is not well established.

  • Book

    Kirkpatrick, D. L., & Kirkpatrick, J. D. (2006). Evaluating Training Programs: The Four Levels (3rd ed.). Berrett-Koehler.

    Used for

    A shared vocabulary for talking about evaluation with institutional stakeholders who already use it.

    Where it is contested

    Holton (1996) argued that the four levels form a taxonomy of outcomes rather than a model, because the causal relationships between levels are assumed rather than specified or tested.

  • Article

    Ladson-Billings, G. (1995). “Toward a Theory of Culturally Relevant Pedagogy.” American Educational Research Journal, 32(3), 465–491.

    Used for

    Culturally relevant pedagogy, used in the equity-centred design resource where accessibility and cultural responsiveness meet.

    Where it is contested

    Developed from a study of eight teachers of African American students in one United States district; extension to other populations and settings requires argument rather than assumption.

5 works

Institutions, change and knowledge use

Why capability built in a workshop does not reach practice, and the frameworks used to close that distance.

  • ArticleOpen access

    Michie, S., van Stralen, M. M., & West, R. (2011). “The Behaviour Change Wheel: A New Method for Characterising and Designing Behaviour Change Interventions.” Implementation Science, 6:42.

    Used for

    COM-B, used to establish whether a gap between accessibility knowledge and accessible practice is capability, opportunity or motivation before anything is designed.

    Where it is contested

    COM-B organises determinants rather than predicting which intervention will work, so the diagnosis it supports does not by itself select a remedy.

  • Book

    Kotter, J. P. (1996). Leading Change. Harvard Business School Press.

    Used for

    A vocabulary for institutional change that senior stakeholders already share, used for communication rather than as a method.

    Where it is contested

    Appelbaum et al. (2012) found the model derives from Kotter's own practitioner observation and that no formal study validates the eight steps as a whole.

  • Book

    Pfeffer, J., & Sutton, R. I. (2000). The Knowing-Doing Gap: How Smart Companies Turn Knowledge into Action. Harvard Business School Press.

    Used for

    Frames why faculty development changes what people know without changing what they build, which is the problem the capacity resource addresses.

    Where it is contested

    The evidence is corporate case material selected to illustrate the argument, and the mechanisms proposed were not tested against alternatives.

  • Article

    Graham, I. D., Logan, J., Harrison, M. B., Straus, S. E., Tetroe, J., Caswell, W., & Robinson, N. (2006). “Lost in Knowledge Translation: Time for a Map?” Journal of Continuing Education in the Health Professions, 26(1), 13–24.

    Used for

    The knowledge-to-action cycle, used for moving published method into institutional practice.

    Where it is contested

    Developed in health services, where a defined practice community and an identifiable clinical decision exist; teaching has neither in the same form.

  • Book

    Senge, P. M. (1990). The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization. Doubleday.

    Used for

    Systems framing for institutional change, used where a barrier recurs because the structure producing it was never addressed.

    Where it is contested

    The five disciplines are presented as an integrated account but have not been validated as one, and the book is practitioner literature rather than research.

9 works

Health, equity and population data

Classification, prevalence, health disparity and the social determinants that situate disability alongside other structural conditions.

  • StandardOpen access

    World Health Organization (2001). International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF). WHO.

    Used for

    The standard vocabulary for describing functioning in relation to a context, and the basis for reasoning from functional impact rather than from diagnosis.

    Where it is contested

    Criticised for a classification complex enough to impede implementation, and for a structure that some argue reinstates a clinical frame under a biopsychosocial name.

  • Report

    World Health Organization & World Bank (2011). World Report on Disability. World Health Organization.

    Used for

    The first global synthesis of prevalence and barriers, cited for historical framing.

    Where it is contested

    Published in 2011 and superseded in several respects, including the headline prevalence estimate, which later WHO reporting revised upward.

  • Report

    World Health Organization (2022). Global Report on Health Equity for Persons with Disabilities. World Health Organization.

    Used for

    The current global estimate and the health-equity framing used where disability is situated as a population-level concern.

    Where it is contested

    Prevalence estimates depend on the instrument used to identify disability, so the figure is not directly comparable with national survey figures.

  • Data

    Statistics Canada. Canadian Survey on Disability.

    Used for

    The national source for Canadian prevalence and for the barrier types disabled Canadians report encountering.

    Where it is contested

    Disability is identified through a screening question set, so the resulting figure reflects that instrument's definition and cannot be set beside figures produced by other national surveys.

  • Article

    Krahn, G. L., Walker, D. K., & Correa-De-Araujo, R. (2015). “Persons with Disabilities as an Unrecognized Health Disparity Population.” American Journal of Public Health, 105(S2), S198–S206.

    Used for

    The argument that disabled people constitute a health disparity population, used in the health and patient education resource.

    Where it is contested

    United States data and United States policy context; several of the disparities identified are mediated by insurance arrangements that do not exist in the same form in Canada.

  • Article

    O’Brien, K. K., Bayoumi, A. M., Strike, C., Young, N. L., & Davis, A. M. (2008). “Exploring Disability from the Perspective of Adults Living with HIV/AIDS: Development of a Conceptual Framework.” Health and Quality of Life Outcomes, 6:76.

    Used for

    The episodic disability framework, and the basis for designing for conditions that fluctuate rather than persist at a constant level.

    Where it is contested

    Derived from one condition and a small qualitative sample, so extension to other episodic conditions proceeds by analogy.

  • Article

    Nutbeam, D. (2000). “Health Literacy as a Public Health Goal: A Challenge for Contemporary Health Education and Communication Strategies into the 21st Century.” Health Promotion International, 15(3), 259–267.

    Used for

    Health literacy as an outcome of education rather than a fixed attribute of a patient, used in patient and public education work.

    Where it is contested

    The three-level model is conceptual, and the measurement of health literacy remains contested, with instruments capturing reading and numeracy more readily than the critical level the model describes.

  • Report

    Meeks, L. M., & Jain, N. R. (2018). Accessibility, Inclusion, and Action in Medical Education: Lived Experiences of Learners and Physicians with Disabilities. Association of American Medical Colleges.

    Used for

    Lived experience of disabled learners and physicians, and the treatment of technical standards in clinical education.

    Where it is contested

    United States medical education, where accreditation requirements and the drafting of technical standards differ from Canadian practice.

  • Report

    WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health (2008). Closing the Gap in a Generation: Health Equity Through Action on the Social Determinants of Health. World Health Organization.

    Used for

    Situates disability alongside the other structural conditions that distribute health, which is the framing the health resources use.

    Where it is contested

    Published in 2008, and its recommendations are addressed to states, so they translate only indirectly into what an institution can decide.
